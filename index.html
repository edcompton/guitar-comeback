Where you're starting

Player profile

💪 Strengths (dusty, not gone)

  • Serious fingerstyle chops — Kensington Blues is American Primitive repertoire; that's an advanced calling card.
  • Alternating-thumb / Travis picking — Van Ronk, Rev. Gary Davis, Blackbird, These Days all live in your hands.
  • Open & altered tunings — the Jack Rose work means re-tuning holds no fear. Slide will feel like home fast.
  • Deep well of taste — Delta & gospel blues through to garage rock, with a serious funk-and-soul streak in your Spotify likes (The Meters, Booker T., Delvon Lamarr). Taste is the hard part; you have it.

🌱 Growth edges

  • Pick technique — flatpicking doesn't feel intuitive yet; shuffle rhythm and riff accuracy need reps.
  • Electric vocabulary — bends, vibrato, pentatonic lead, improvising over a 12-bar.
  • Slide — brand new voice: intonation over the fret, muting behind the slide.
  • Consistency — the real boss fight. The plan is built around this, not around technique.

🎸 Give every guitar a job

  • Martin — standard tuning, the fingerstyle & folk-blues machine. Repertoire revival lives here.
  • Epiphone Dove — lives in open D as the dedicated slide guitar. Two acoustics means one never needs re-tuning — zero-friction slide practice. (Swap roles if the Martin takes a slide better.)
  • Stratocaster + Bassbreaker — stays plugged in, volume pre-set, pick on the amp. From "I fancy it" to first riff in under 20 seconds.

Anchor · Tuesday

🔥 Electric Blues Lab

Strat + Bassbreaker. 10 min technique (shuffle rhythm, palm mutes, pentatonic licks — JustinGuitar material), then 15+ min applying it: riff songs and improvising over a 12-bar backing track. This is where pick confidence gets built.

Anchor · Thursday

🎯 New Song Workshop

One current build song from the pipeline. Slow, sectional work — the same way you built Kensington Blues. One song at a time until it's yours.

🪕

Repertoire Revival

Martin, standard tuning. Run 2–3 old songs; one "focus song of the week" gets polished back to performance level.

🥃

Slide School

The Dove, already in open D. Ten minutes of technique, then licks and song work — Dust My Broom, Death Letter.

🎧

Free Play

No goals. Play along with records, noodle, improvise, play the hits. Counts fully. At least one of these a week — it's the point of the whole thing.

Anchor days are suggestions — move them to whatever evenings are calmest. The rule is only: two anchors, every week.

The arc

Twelve weeks, three phases

Loose targets, not deadlines. If a phase takes five weeks instead of four, nothing breaks. The only failure mode is stopping.

IReconnectWeeks 1–4

Get hands and habit back

  • Revive one old song per week: Blackbird → These Days → Hang Me, Oh Hang Me → Fare Thee Well. Calluses and thumb groove return here.
  • Electric re-entry: JustinGuitar's Blues Guitar & Easy Improvisation refresh — 12-bar shuffle rhythm, minor pentatonic pattern 1, first improv over a backing track. Plus Seven Nation Army for pure fun.
  • Slide basics on the open-D Dove: intonation directly over the fret wire, muting behind the slide, slow one-string melodies.
  • Milestone: phone-record one revived song at the end of week 4.
IIBuildWeeks 5–8

New vocabulary on every guitar

  • Revive the meatier pieces: Green Green Rocky Road, Death Don't Have No Mercy. Start first new build: Silver Joy or The Partisan.
  • Start Essential Blues Lead Guitar: bends, vibrato, first licks. Riff songs: I Got Mine, Little Black Submarines — and Cissy Strut as funk pick-hand bootcamp.
  • The Dust My Broom lick until it sings; first verse of Death Letter.
  • Milestone: record an electric riff take at the end of week 8.
IIIStretchWeeks 9–12

The capstones

  • Rebuild Kensington Blues to full performance. It took months the first time; it'll take weeks now.
  • Improv fluency: full 12-bar solos over backing tracks. Stretch goal: the Little Wing intro from your saved tabs.
  • Death Letter start to finish; add slide colour to Have Mercy on Me.
  • Milestone: record the Living Room EP.

Track colours: acoustic electric slide

🎙️ The Living Room EP — the week-12 finish line

Five phone recordings, one sitting or several: a revived fingerstyle piece · a brand-new song · an electric riff track · a slide tune · one wildcard. Nobody ever needs to hear it. It exists so you can hear the comeback.

Making it stick

Rules of the game

Guitars live on stands, Strat stays plugged in.Cases are where habits die. The distance between "I fancy it" and playing should be under 20 seconds.
When the gaming urge hits, pick up a guitar first.Ten minutes of playing buys anything else guilt-free. Most nights, you won't want to stop — you already know this about yourself.
Minimum viable session: 10 minutes. Never zero, never miss twice.One song counts. Consistency beats intensity every single week of this plan.
End on something that sounds great.The last thing your brain files away should be "that was satisfying", not "I kept fluffing bar 12".
Scales serve songs.JustinGuitar drills are capped at 10 minutes per session. The moment theory starts killing the buzz, go play Seven Nation Army at full volume.
Rusty is not lost.A song that took six weeks to learn takes days to revive. The first fumbling run-through is the worst it will ever sound again.
Record at every milestone.Phone on the bookshelf, one take, no editing. Hearing yourself back is where the love gets rebuilt.
